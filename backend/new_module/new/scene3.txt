==============================
SCENARIO: Wildfire Mitigation Policy Discussion
==============================

[Scene Layer]

Context Objective:
- A group engages in a collaborative policy discussion to recommend a wildfire mitigation policy direction for a resource-constrained regional government.

Decision Nature:
- Single structured discussion session.
- Participants evaluate and compare policy-level directions.
- The group must reach a shared policy recommendation with explicit trade-off acknowledgment.

Constraints:
- Limited public funding: not all strategies can be fully funded simultaneously.
- Operational capacity is finite: prioritization is required.
- Trade-offs between public safety, environmental protection, and economic impact must be surfaced explicitly.
- Policies may produce both short-term and long-term consequences that differ significantly.

Option Space (policy directions only — do not expand beyond these):
- Land management interventions (e.g. controlled burns, mechanical thinning)
- Infrastructure investment (e.g. firebreaks, evacuation routes, detection systems)
- Regulatory measures (e.g. zoning, land-use restrictions, building codes)
- Mixed policy package (combination of the above with defined priorities and trade-offs)

Temporal Boundary:
- A final policy direction must be produced within the session timeframe.


[Fact Layer]

Wildfire Mitigation Strategies:

Land Management:
- Controlled burns: reduces fuel load, ecologically complex, requires expertise
- Mechanical thinning: effective but resource-intensive
- Both require ongoing maintenance, not one-time interventions

Infrastructure:
- Early detection systems: high upfront cost, reduces response time
- Firebreak construction: effective buffer but disrupts land use
- Evacuation infrastructure: critical for safety, long lead time

Regulatory:
- Zoning restrictions: prevents future risk accumulation, economically contentious
- Building codes: long-term risk reduction, affects development costs

Trade-off Patterns:
- Immediate risk reduction vs long-term ecological balance
- Environmental protection vs economic activity
- Upfront investment vs reactive cost
- Central coordination vs local flexibility
- Prevention-focused vs response-focused


[Domain Output Policy Layer]

Scope Boundary (strictly enforced):
- Discuss ONLY policy directions and their trade-offs.
- Do NOT discuss implementation details, outreach campaigns, community engagement activities, social media, education programs, or public awareness initiatives unless a policy direction has already been agreed upon.
- Do NOT introduce new policy categories outside the Option Space above.
- If the conversation drifts to implementation or community activities, redirect it back to policy-level comparison.

Discussion Guidelines:
- Do not assume a single "correct" policy.
- Avoid framing any strategy as morally superior.
- Surface trade-offs explicitly when comparing options.
- Each response should either introduce a new comparison dimension, challenge an existing trade-off, or move toward narrowing the options.

Information Use:
- Reference the mitigation strategies in the Fact Layer when helpful.
- Avoid introducing external political ideology.
- Do not invent statistics or cite specific studies.

Decision Support:
- The goal is a concrete shared recommendation: one policy direction or a defined mixed package with explicit priorities.
- Vague conclusions ("we should consider all options") are not acceptable as a final output.
