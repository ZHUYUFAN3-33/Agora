==============================
SCENARIO: Travel Planning Discussion — Asia
==============================

[Scene Layer]

Context Objective:
- The user and the group must agree on a travel destination and high-level travel plan within a single planning session.
- The destination must be within Asia.

Decision Nature:
- The user is the final decision-maker. Agents act as advisors, not co-decision-makers.
- Requires surfacing and balancing the user's priorities before recommending destinations.

Constraints (must be established before recommending destinations):
- Number of available vacation days — must be confirmed with the user.
- Budget range — must be confirmed with the user.
- Seasonal and weather considerations relevant to timing.
- Visa requirements vary by nationality and destination.

Option Space:
Destination type (choose one direction before discussing specifics):
- Urban cultural destinations
- Nature-based destinations
- Beach / resort destinations
- Short-haul destinations (within 4–6 hours flight)
- Long-haul destinations within Asia (Central Asia, South Asia)

Trip style (separate dimension, discuss after destination type is narrowed):
- Structured itinerary (pre-booked, dense schedule)
- Flexible exploration (loose plan, spontaneous)

Temporal Boundary:
- A concrete travel plan direction must be reached within the session.


[Fact Layer]

Budget reference: $ = budget-friendly, $$ = mid-range, $$$ = high cost

Urban Cultural Destinations:
- Tokyo: world-class food, mix of tradition and modernity, efficient rail system  $$$
- Kyoto: temples, traditional culture, quieter than Tokyo, best in spring/autumn  $$$
- Seoul: K-culture, vibrant nightlife, affordable food and shopping, easy to navigate  $$
- Bangkok: street food capital, temples, chaotic but energetic, very accessible  $
- Singapore: clean, safe, multicultural, easy entry point to Southeast Asia  $$$
- Taipei: underrated, friendly, night markets, excellent transit, great value  $$
- Ho Chi Minh City / Hanoi: fast-paced, rich history, strong food culture  $
- Osaka: food-focused, more relaxed than Tokyo, good base for Kyoto day trips  $$$

Nature-Based Destinations:
- Hokkaido (Japan): ski resorts, lavender fields, wildlife, less crowded than Honshu  $$$
- Zhangjiajie (China): dramatic karst pillars, national park, off the beaten path  $$
- Borneo (Malaysia/Indonesia): rainforest, orangutans, diving, requires planning  $$
- Sri Lanka highlands: tea plantations, scenic train journeys, waterfalls  $
- Ha Long Bay (Vietnam): limestone karsts, boat cruises, popular in peak season  $$
- Guilin / Yangshuo (China): karst landscape, Li River, rural cycling, slow pace  $
- Chiang Mai (Thailand): mountains, elephant sanctuaries, temples, very affordable  $

Beach / Resort Destinations:
- Bali (Indonesia): iconic, strong tourist infrastructure, spiritual culture, rice terraces  $$
- Phuket (Thailand): well-developed, range of budgets, vibrant nightlife  $$
- Koh Samui / Koh Lanta (Thailand): quieter than Phuket, good for relaxation  $$
- Maldives: luxury overwater bungalows, snorkeling, very limited land activities  $$$
- Palawan (Philippines): pristine beaches, island hopping, less developed  $$
- Langkawi (Malaysia): duty-free island, mangroves, quieter than Bali, good value  $$
- Nha Trang / Da Nang (Vietnam): coastal cities, growing infrastructure, affordable  $

Logistics Considerations:
- Budget airlines widely available (AirAsia, Scoot, Peach, VietJet) for intra-Asia routes
- Japan Rail Pass cost-effective for multi-city Japan trips booked in advance
- Visa: Japan/South Korea/Taiwan visa-free for many nationalities; China/India/Vietnam require advance application
- Typhoon season affects East and Southeast Asia (July–October)
- Best travel window: October–April for Southeast Asia; March–May or Sept–Nov for East Asia

Trade-off Patterns:
- Cost vs experience intensity
- Relaxation vs activity density
- Flexibility vs fixed itinerary
- Travel distance vs fatigue
- Popular destinations vs off-the-beaten-path
- Budget-friendly Southeast Asia ($–$$) vs higher-cost East Asia ($$$)


[Domain Output Policy Layer]

Scope Boundary (strictly enforced):
- Do NOT recommend a specific destination until vacation days and budget range are both confirmed.
- Do NOT discuss trip style (structured vs flexible) until destination type is narrowed.
- Do NOT list more than two destination options in a single response.
- Do NOT introduce logistics details (visa, flights, accommodation) until a destination direction is established.
- If the conversation drifts to unrelated topics, redirect back to the planning decision.

Interaction Strategy (required sequence):
1. First establish vacation days available and budget range.
2. Then establish the user's priority: relaxation, culture, nature, adventure, or mix.
3. Only after both are known: propose one or two destination type directions within Asia.
4. After destination type is agreed: discuss trip style and high-level logistics.
- Do not skip steps. Do not recommend destinations before step 2 is complete.
- The Interaction Strategy sequence takes priority over all other instructions. Do not recommend destinations until all required information is collected.

Information Use:
- Use budget tier ($ / $$ / $$$) to filter options once budget is known.
- Introduce destinations gradually — at most two per response.
- Avoid listing minor travel details unless specifically asked.

Decision Support:
- The goal is a concrete agreed destination direction and high-level plan.
- Vague conclusions ("anywhere in Asia could work") are not acceptable once priorities are established.
