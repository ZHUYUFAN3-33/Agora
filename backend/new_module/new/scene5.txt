==============================
SCENARIO: Travel Planning Discussion — Europe
==============================

[Scene Layer]

Context Objective:
- The user and the group must agree on a travel destination and high-level travel plan within a single planning session.
- The destination must be within Europe.

Decision Nature:
- The user is the final decision-maker. Agents act as advisors, not co-decision-makers.
- Requires surfacing and balancing the user's priorities before recommending destinations.

Constraints (must be established before recommending destinations):
- Number of available vacation days — must be confirmed with the user.
- Budget range — must be confirmed with the user.
- Seasonal and weather considerations relevant to timing.
- Schengen visa requirements depending on the user's nationality.

Option Space:
Destination type (choose one direction before discussing specifics):
- Urban cultural destinations
- Nature-based destinations
- Beach / resort destinations
- Single-country deep dive vs multi-country trip
- Western Europe (higher cost) vs Eastern Europe (lower cost, fewer crowds)

Trip style (separate dimension, discuss after destination type is narrowed):
- Structured itinerary (pre-booked, dense schedule)
- Flexible exploration (Interrail / road trip style)

Temporal Boundary:
- A concrete travel plan direction must be reached within the session.


[Fact Layer]

Budget reference: $ = budget-friendly, $$ = mid-range, $$$ = high cost

Urban Cultural Destinations:
- Paris: iconic landmarks, world-class museums, food culture, very crowded in summer  $$$
- Rome: ancient history, Vatican, street food, chaotic but rewarding  $$
- Barcelona: Gaudí architecture, beaches, nightlife, tapas, warm climate  $$
- Amsterdam: canals, cycling culture, Rijksmuseum, compact and walkable  $$$
- Prague: stunning old town, medieval architecture, beer culture  $
- Vienna: classical music, imperial palaces, coffee house culture, organized  $$
- Lisbon: trams, fado music, seafood, one of the more affordable Western capitals  $$
- Dubrovnik: walled old city, Adriatic views, very crowded in peak season  $$
- Budapest: thermal baths, ruin bars, grand architecture, divided by the Danube  $

Nature-Based Destinations:
- Norwegian fjords: dramatic landscapes, fjord cruises, midnight sun in summer  $$$
- Scottish Highlands: castles, whisky distilleries, road trip friendly, unpredictable weather  $$
- Dolomites (Italy/Austria): mountain scenery, hiking in summer, skiing in winter  $$
- Black Forest (Germany): forests, cycling trails, fairy-tale villages  $$
- Plitvice Lakes (Croatia): cascading turquoise lakes, UNESCO site, book early  $$
- Faroe Islands: remote, dramatic cliffs and waterfalls, few tourists  $$$
- Swiss Alps (Interlaken / Zermatt): stunning but among the most expensive in Europe  $$$

Beach / Resort Destinations:
- Santorini (Greece): iconic cliffs, sunsets, volcanic beaches, very expensive in peak season  $$$
- Mykonos (Greece): nightlife, beaches, cosmopolitan atmosphere  $$$
- Amalfi Coast (Italy): dramatic coastal road, colorful villages, best by boat or bus  $$$
- Algarve (Portugal): sea stacks, golden beaches, surfing, more affordable than Mediterranean  $$
- Mallorca (Spain): beaches and mountains, range of budgets, popular with families  $$
- Hvar (Croatia): upscale island, lavender fields, clear water, sailing hub  $$
- Montenegro coast: emerging destination, dramatic scenery, quieter than Croatia  $

Logistics Considerations:
- Budget airlines widely available (Ryanair, EasyJet, Wizz Air) for intra-Europe routes
- Eurail pass useful for multi-country trips; point-to-point tickets often cheaper booked early
- Peak season (June–August) significantly increases cost and crowds
- Shoulder season (April–May, September–October) better value and more pleasant
- Schengen zone allows borderless travel across most of Western and Central Europe
- Eastern Europe significantly cheaper than Western Europe across all categories

Trade-off Patterns:
- Cost vs experience intensity
- Single destination depth vs multi-country breadth
- Relaxation vs activity density
- Flexibility vs fixed itinerary
- Western Europe familiarity ($$$) vs Eastern Europe value ($)
- Peak season crowds vs shoulder season availability


[Domain Output Policy Layer]

Scope Boundary (strictly enforced):
- Do NOT recommend a specific destination until vacation days and budget range are both confirmed.
- Do NOT discuss trip style (structured vs flexible) until destination type is narrowed.
- Do NOT list more than two destination options in a single response.
- Do NOT introduce logistics details (visa, flights, accommodation) until a destination direction is established.
- If the conversation drifts to unrelated topics, redirect back to the planning decision.

Interaction Strategy (required sequence):
1. First establish vacation days available and budget range.
2. Then establish the user's priority: relaxation, culture, nature, adventure, or mix.
3. Only after both are known: propose one or two destination type directions within Europe.
4. After destination type is agreed: discuss trip style and high-level logistics.
- Do not skip steps. Do not recommend destinations before step 2 is complete.
- The Interaction Strategy sequence takes priority over all other instructions. Do not recommend destinations until all required information is collected.

Information Use:
- Use budget tier ($ / $$ / $$$) to filter options once budget is known.
- Introduce destinations gradually — at most two per response.
- Avoid listing minor travel details unless specifically asked.

Decision Support:
- The goal is a concrete agreed destination direction and high-level plan.
- Vague conclusions ("anywhere in Europe could work") are not acceptable once priorities are established.
