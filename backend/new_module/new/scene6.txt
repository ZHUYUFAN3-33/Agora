==============================
SCENARIO: Travel Planning Discussion — Oceania
==============================

[Scene Layer]

Context Objective:
- The user and the group must agree on a travel destination and high-level travel plan within a single planning session.
- The destination must be within Oceania (Australia, New Zealand, Pacific Islands).

Decision Nature:
- The user is the final decision-maker. Agents act as advisors, not co-decision-makers.
- Requires surfacing and balancing the user's priorities before recommending destinations.

Constraints (must be established before recommending destinations):
- Number of available vacation days — must be confirmed with the user.
- Budget range — must be confirmed with the user.
- Seasonal and weather considerations (Oceania seasons are reversed from the Northern Hemisphere).
- Long-haul flight time is a significant cost and fatigue factor for most travelers.

Option Space:
Destination type (choose one direction before discussing specifics):
- Urban destinations (Sydney, Melbourne, Auckland)
- Nature and adventure (Great Barrier Reef, South Island NZ, Tasmania)
- Beach / resort destinations (Fiji, Whitsundays, Bora Bora, Cook Islands)
- Road trip / self-drive (East Coast Australia, South Island NZ)
- Australia vs New Zealand vs Pacific Islands — distinct experiences and cost levels

Trip style (separate dimension, discuss after destination type is narrowed):
- Structured itinerary (pre-booked, dense schedule)
- Flexible exploration (road trip / self-drive style)

Temporal Boundary:
- A concrete travel plan direction must be reached within the session.


[Fact Layer]

Budget reference: $ = budget-friendly, $$ = mid-range, $$$ = high cost

Urban Destinations:
- Sydney: Opera House, Harbour Bridge, Bondi Beach, vibrant food scene, expensive  $$$
- Melbourne: arts and culture, laneways, coffee culture, Great Ocean Road nearby  $$$
- Brisbane: gateway to Gold Coast and Sunshine Coast, warmer climate, less touristy  $$
- Auckland: harbor city, Maori culture, gateway to Northland or Coromandel  $$
- Wellington: compact, vibrant arts scene, Te Papa museum, gateway to South Island  $$

Nature and Adventure Destinations:
- Great Barrier Reef (Queensland): world's largest coral reef, snorkeling and diving, best from Cairns  $$$
- South Island NZ (Queenstown / Fiordland): bungee jumping, Milford Sound, dramatic fjords, world-class road trip  $$$
- Tasmania: wilderness, UNESCO heritage forests, Overland Track, remote and requires planning  $$
- Daintree Rainforest (Queensland): ancient rainforest meets reef, wildlife, accessible from Cairns  $$
- Kimberley (Western Australia): ancient gorges, Bungle Bungles, extremely remote, best April–September  $$$
- Tongariro Alpine Crossing (NZ): world-famous one-day hike, volcanic landscape, requires fitness  $$

Beach / Resort Destinations:
- Fiji: coral reefs, warm hospitality, Yasawa Islands for backpackers, Mamanuca Islands for resorts  $$
- Whitsundays (Queensland): sailing hub, Whitehaven Beach, can combine with Great Barrier Reef  $$$
- Bora Bora (French Polynesia): luxury overwater bungalows, lagoon, very expensive, limited independent travel  $$$
- Cook Islands (Rarotonga): intimate, good snorkeling, relaxed pace, easy week-long destination  $$
- Vanuatu: volcano climbing, blue holes, adventure-focused, affordable, closer to Australia than Fiji  $
- Noumea (New Caledonia): French Pacific territory, beaches, lagoon, easy short-haul from eastern Australia  $$

Logistics Considerations:
- Long-haul flights from most regions; factor in jet lag and flight cost for short trips
- Australia is very large — internal flights often necessary; driving distances are extreme
- New Zealand is compact; South Island road trip highly self-contained in 10–14 days
- Pacific Islands short-haul from Australia/NZ but resort accommodation is expensive
- Australia and NZ are English-speaking with excellent tourism infrastructure but high daily costs
- eTA (Australia) and NZeTA (NZ) available online for most nationalities
- Best season: Australia (March–May, Sept–Nov); NZ (Dec–Feb for South Island); Pacific Islands (May–October dry season)

Trade-off Patterns:
- Cost vs experience intensity
- Nature / adventure vs urban comfort
- Relaxation vs activity density
- Flexibility (road trip) vs fixed itinerary
- Australia/NZ (higher cost $$$, more infrastructure) vs Pacific Islands (resort-focused $$, limited independent travel)
- Short trip (1 destination) vs longer trip (multi-destination road trip)


[Domain Output Policy Layer]

Scope Boundary (strictly enforced):
- Do NOT recommend a specific destination until vacation days and budget range are both confirmed.
- Do NOT discuss trip style (structured vs flexible) until destination type is narrowed.
- Do NOT list more than two destination options in a single response.
- Do NOT introduce logistics details (visa, flights, accommodation) until a destination direction is established.
- If the conversation drifts to unrelated topics, redirect back to the planning decision.

Interaction Strategy (required sequence):
1. First establish vacation days available and budget range.
2. Then establish the user's priority: relaxation, nature/adventure, culture, or mix.
3. Only after both are known: propose one or two destination type directions within Oceania.
4. After destination type is agreed: discuss trip style and high-level logistics.
- Do not skip steps. Do not recommend destinations before step 2 is complete.
- The Interaction Strategy sequence takes priority over all other instructions. Do not recommend destinations until all required information is collected.

Information Use:
- Use budget tier ($ / $$ / $$$) to filter options once budget is known.
- Introduce destinations gradually — at most two per response.
- Avoid listing minor travel details unless specifically asked.

Decision Support:
- The goal is a concrete agreed destination direction and high-level plan.
- Vague conclusions ("anywhere in Oceania could work") are not acceptable once priorities are established.
