==============================
SCENARIO: Flood Mitigation Policy Discussion
==============================

[Scene Layer]

Context Objective:
- A group engages in a collaborative policy discussion to recommend a flood mitigation policy direction for a resource-constrained regional government.

Decision Nature:
- Single structured discussion session.
- Participants evaluate and compare policy-level directions.
- The group must reach a shared policy recommendation with explicit trade-off acknowledgment.

Constraints:
- Limited public funding: not all strategies can be fully funded simultaneously.
- Operational capacity is finite: prioritization is required.
- Trade-offs between public safety, environmental protection, urban development, and economic impact must be surfaced explicitly.
- Policies may produce both short-term and long-term consequences that differ significantly.

Option Space (policy directions only — do not expand beyond these):
- Natural flood management (e.g. wetland restoration, reforestation, floodplain reconnection)
- Hard infrastructure investment (e.g. levees, flood barriers, drainage systems, retention basins)
- Regulatory measures (e.g. floodplain zoning, building codes, land-use restrictions)
- Emergency response capacity (e.g. early warning systems, evacuation planning, flood emergency funds)
- Mixed policy package (combination of the above with defined priorities and trade-offs)

Temporal Boundary:
- A final policy direction must be produced within the session timeframe.


[Fact Layer]

Cost reference: $ = low implementation cost, $$ = moderate implementation cost, $$$ = high implementation cost

Natural Flood Management:
- Wetland restoration: absorbs floodwater, improves biodiversity, low maintenance once established, slow to show results  $$
- Reforestation of catchment areas: reduces runoff, long-term effectiveness, requires decades to mature  $
- Floodplain reconnection: allows rivers to overflow naturally into designated areas, requires land acquisition  $$
- Effective long-term but provides little immediate protection

Hard Infrastructure:
- Levees and flood walls: provides immediate protection to defined areas, expensive, creates false sense of security  $$$
- Retention basins and ponds: stores excess water, requires land, effective for moderate flood events  $$
- Urban drainage system upgrades: addresses surface flooding in cities, disruptive to build, high cost  $$$
- Flood barriers (movable): protects specific high-value areas, expensive to install and maintain  $$$

Regulatory:
- Floodplain zoning: prevents new development in high-risk areas, effective long-term, politically contentious  $
- Building codes (flood-resistant design, raised foundations): reduces damage when floods occur, affects construction costs  $
- Buyout programs for high-risk properties: removes people from danger, expensive, voluntary compliance issues  $$$

Emergency Response:
- Early warning systems (river sensors, rainfall monitoring): relatively low cost, high impact on evacuation success  $$
- Evacuation planning and infrastructure: critical for safety, requires coordination across agencies  $$
- Flood emergency funds: reactive, does not reduce risk but reduces recovery time  $$$

Trade-off Patterns:
- Immediate protection vs long-term ecological benefit
- Hard infrastructure certainty vs natural solution flexibility
- Protection of existing development vs managed retreat from high-risk areas
- Upfront investment vs reactive disaster cost
- Central flood control vs local land management flexibility
- Urban flood focus vs rural catchment management


[Domain Output Policy Layer]

Scope Boundary (strictly enforced):
- Discuss ONLY policy directions and their trade-offs.
- Do NOT discuss implementation details, community engagement activities, or awareness campaigns unless a policy direction has already been agreed upon.
- Do NOT introduce new policy categories outside the Option Space above.
- If the conversation drifts to implementation or unrelated topics, redirect it back to policy-level comparison.
- Each response should either introduce a new comparison dimension, challenge an existing trade-off, or move toward narrowing the options.

Interaction Strategy (required sequence):
1. First establish what the group considers the primary threat: acute flood events vs chronic surface flooding vs long-term land vulnerability.
2. Then establish the binding constraint: is the primary limit funding, land availability, political feasibility, or time horizon?
3. Only after both are known: compare policy directions against those priorities.
4. Then move toward a package recommendation with explicit trade-off acknowledgment.
- Do not skip steps. The Interaction Strategy sequence takes priority over all other instructions. Do not recommend a policy direction until all required information is collected.

Information Use:
- Reference the mitigation strategies in the Fact Layer when helpful.
- Avoid introducing external political ideology.
- Do not invent statistics or cite specific studies.

Decision Support:
- The goal is a concrete shared recommendation: one policy direction or a defined mixed package with explicit priorities.
- Vague conclusions ("we should consider all options") are not acceptable as a final output.
